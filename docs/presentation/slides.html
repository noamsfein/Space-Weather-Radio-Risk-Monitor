The problem, the user, the result

Presentation rubric 1 — problem, user, useful result, why it matters

  • User: an amateur-radio operator who relies on HF propagation.
  • Problem: geomagnetic storms degrade HF; NOAA's Kp estimate updates every minute, but nobody wants to watch a feed.
  • Rule: alert when the rolling 15-minute max Kp crosses from below 6 to ≥ 6 (G2 storm — NOAA: HF can fade at higher latitudes). Rearm only after it falls below 6.
Result: a validated Kafka stream turns 9 replayed observations into exactly 2 machine-readable alerts — no spam while conditions stay elevated, no AI in the decision.

NOAA source → one validated event

Presentation rubric 2 — source, Kafka event, key fields, representative record

  • Source: NOAA SWPC planetary_k_index_1m.json — public, no key. Values are estimated, preliminary, global.
  • Raw estimated_kp → canonical kp_value; every event passes one Pydantic contract (UTC times, Kp in 0–9, documented provenance only).
  • Topic kp_observations, constant key planetary_kp: one global sequence stays ordered. Dedup by time_tag.
  • Quiet feed (checked Aug 11 + 12: max Kp < 3.5) → a labeled synthetic fixture is the required alert demo.

Lesson, limitation, next step

Presentation rubric 4 — evidence-based lesson and a realistic next step

  • Lesson: deterministic replay beat live data for proving stream logic — a quiet feed can never show a threshold crossing, but a labeled fixture shows crossing, dedup, and rearm every single run.
  • Limitation: this reports a recent estimated global Kp condition — not a forecast, not a station-level propagation model; the validator checks facts, not eloquence.
  • Next step: the deferred 60-second live NOAA poller — the contract, topic, and consumer already accept normalized NOAA records unchanged.
